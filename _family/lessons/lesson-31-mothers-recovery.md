# Lesson 31: Mother's Physical Recovery

{: .important }
> General education, not medical advice. Postpartum warning signs can appear for
> **up to a year** — when in doubt, call. See the red-flag box below.

{: .note }
> **Words to know**
> - **postpartum** (post-PAR-tum) — the period after birth; the "fourth trimester" is roughly the first 3 months.
> - **lochia** (LOH-kee-ah) — the normal vaginal bleeding/discharge after birth, lasting weeks.
> - **afterpains** — cramps as the womb shrinks back down, often stronger during breastfeeding.
> - **perineum** (pair-ih-NEE-um) — the area that may have torn or been cut and stitched.
> - **pelvic floor** — the muscles supporting the bladder/womb; birth can weaken them.
> - **6-week check** — a postnatal checkup for the mother around six weeks after birth.

## Concept

Birth is a major physical event, and recovery is **real, gradual, and often
underestimated**. The culture of "bouncing back" does new mothers a disservice —
the body needs **weeks to months**, not days. Knowing what's normal (bleeding,
cramps, soreness, exhaustion) and what's a **warning sign** lets the mother recover
without fear, and lets the partner protect that recovery.

## Going Deeper

### What's normal in recovery

- **Lochia** — vaginal bleeding that's heavier at first and tapers over several
  weeks (like a long period, gradually lightening).
- **Afterpains** — cramps as the uterus shrinks; often stronger during
  breastfeeding (that's the hormones doing their job).
- **Perineal soreness or stitches** (after a tear/episiotomy) — keep the area clean;
  ice, sitz baths, and comfort measures help.
- **C-section recovery** (Lesson 22) — incision care, no heavy lifting, gradual
  movement.
- **Other normal changes:** sore/leaking breasts, night sweats, hormonal mood
  shifts, constipation, tiredness, and later some **hair loss** (temporary).

### It takes time — the "fourth trimester"

The **6-week check** is a milestone, **not** a finish line — full recovery
(muscles, pelvic floor, energy, tissues) often takes **months**. Rest, good
nutrition, hydration, and **help with everything else** are the recipe. Discourage
any rush to "get the body back"; healing and caring for a newborn is more than
enough.

### The pelvic floor

Birth can weaken the **pelvic floor**, sometimes causing leaking urine when coughing
or laughing. **Pelvic-floor exercises** help, and persistent problems deserve a
referral to a **pelvic-health physiotherapist** — this is common and treatable, not
something to just live with.

### Warning signs — recovery isn't only the early days

{: .warning }
> **Call for help or seek urgent care (postpartum, up to a year) for:**
> - **Heavy bleeding** — soaking more than one pad an hour, or large clots
> - **Fever**, or a **foul-smelling discharge**
> - **Severe or worsening pain**, or a very painful/hot C-section incision
> - **Chest pain or trouble breathing**, or **pain/redness/swelling in one leg**
>   *(possible blood clot — emergency)*
> - **Severe headache or vision changes** *(blood pressure — preeclampsia can occur
>   after birth too)*
> - **Any thoughts of harming yourself or the baby** *(get help now — Lesson 32)*

{: .note }
> **"Bouncing back" is a myth — recovery is the goal**
> A mother has just done something enormous. Her job now is to **heal and feed the
> baby**, not to hurry her body or resume normal life. Rest, accept help, eat well,
> and give it months. Protecting her recovery is one of the partner's most important
> jobs (Lesson 33).

### Where to start

Make a **recovery plan for the first two weeks** — specific, written down, and
agreed before the birth. Who covers chores, who cooks or organises meals, who
handles older children and pets, and who is "on" at night. The purpose is
singular: to let the mother focus on healing and feeding, and on nothing else.

Both of you should also learn the **postpartum warning signs** and save the
numbers. This matters because postpartum vigilance does not end when you leave
the hospital — serious complications can appear days or even weeks later, at
precisely the point when everyone has decided the dangerous part is over.

The framing to carry: recovery from birth is a **months-long project**, not a
long weekend. Treating it that way protects the mother's health, and it protects
her from the widespread and untrue belief that she should be back to normal
already.
